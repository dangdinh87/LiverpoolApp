Liverpool have £56m transfer decision to make with terms already agreed

Jarell Quansah is attracting the interest of Arsenal and Real Madrid but should Liverpool wish to re-sign the defender from Bayer Leverkusen, a deal is already in place

When Liverpool decided to let Jarell Quansah leave for Bayer Leverkusen in the summer of 2025, the deal came with caveats for the buyers. A £35m agreement was struck with the Bundesliga side to allow the Warrington-born defender the chance to broaden his horizons at a club who had won the German title just two seasons prior, but also leave the door open for his return.

‌
‌

The opportunity to play regular Champions League football appealed to Quansah, who saw game-time at the BayArena as being more regular than it would have been at the time at Anfield under Arne Slot, who preferred Ibrahima Konate as Virgil van Dijk's partner at the back.

‌

Liverpool, though, sold the Academy graduate with an attached buy-back clause and a pre-contract agreed with the player.

That meant that any return for Quansah would be a relatively straightforward process of paying the fee that had been struck in the unusual terms.

There was to be no need for complicated or protracted negotiations with Leverkusen or any concern that the player himself might have wage demands too lofty for the Reds to realistically meet.

Liverpool always believed Quansah had the talent to eventually become an England regular and he has made good on that prediction, starting games at the World Cup and continuing his development during the current Nations League international break.

‌

The right-back or centre-back admitted in April that his choice to move was a relatively straightforward one after Leverkusen offered him regular, first-team football, saying: "To be honest, I wouldn't say it was the hardest decision because I just wanted to play.

"I felt like I could play at the top level, the Bundesliga's a top league and being able to play in the Champions League and play top games.

‌

"I think you just have maybe a gut feeling. Sometimes you can't think about it too much and listen to too many people to be honest, because you can listen to a few things and get persuaded."
